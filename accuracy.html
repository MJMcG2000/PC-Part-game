How accurate is Rigdle's FPS model?

Accuracy study · November 24, 2026

Rigdle doesn't run real benchmarks. It estimates frame rates with a simple model: each CPU and GPU has a relative score, each game has a CPU and a GPU "load", and a build's FPS is the lower of the CPU and GPU numbers. We compared that model with published benchmark results to see how close it gets, and fixed what was off.

How we tested

  • Hardware rankings: how much faster one part is than another in Rigdle, compared with the gaps reported in reviews (e.g. "RTX 4060 vs RTX 3060").
  • Resolution scaling: how much frame rates drop from 1080p to 1440p and from 1440p to 4K, compared with multi-game averages from reviews.
  • Game frame rates: Rigdle's FPS for a specific game and part, compared with that game's benchmark results.

The error is how far Rigdle's number is from the published one, as a percentage. Where sources gave a range, we compared with its middle. Reviews use different scenes, settings and test systems, so a few percent of disagreement between sources is normal; we treat anything within ±10% as accurate for a puzzle game.

What we changed

  • 4K is harder on GPUs: the 4K multiplier went from 0.42 to 0.38 of 1080p performance.
  • RTX 3070 score raised from 119 to 126, matching its gap to the RTX 4070.
  • Cyberpunk 2077 runs a bit faster (GPU load 0.8 → 0.9).
  • Black Myth: Wukong (0.55 → 0.5) and Starfield (0.6 → 0.5) run a bit slower.

These changes reshuffled future puzzles before launch. We left two results flagged rather than "fixing" them on thin evidence: the Ryzen 5 3600 gap comes from a single game, and the Hogwarts Legacy sources don't agree on settings.

What the study doesn't cover

  • Most of the 230 games. We checked five games directly; the rest rely on hand-set loads that follow the same approach. Rigdle uses real benchmark results instead of estimates wherever we add them, and labels each number as "Benchmark" or "Estimate".
  • Console games. They're estimated as played on a PC through an emulator or PC port. Emulator performance varies a lot between versions and games, so these are the roughest numbers in Rigdle.
  • Hardware from 2000 to 2015. These parts can't run the games reviewers test today, so their scores are extrapolated from period reviews rather than checked here.
  • The bottleneck rule. Real PCs slow down a little before one part fully limits the other, and some games need features (like DirectX 12) that old parts lack. Rigdle ignores both to keep puzzles solvable one part at a time.
  • Sources. Figures come from published reviews and, where noted, community or aggregator sites. Settings and test scenes differ between sources.

Rigdle is a game, not a buying guide. If you're choosing parts for a real PC, check full reviews for the games you play. Read more about Rigdle.
