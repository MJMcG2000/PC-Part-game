About Rigdle

A daily puzzle about building the cheapest gaming PC that still does the job.

Every Rigdle puzzle gives you a game, a target frame rate and a resolution, such as Cyberpunk 2077 at 90 FPS in 1440p. Your job is to spec the CPU, graphics card and amount of RAM that get there. The catch is that you're looking for the minimum parts, not the best ones. An RTX 5090 will hit almost any target, but it isn't the answer.

Daily and Endless

Daily mode has one puzzle per day, and everyone gets the same one. It's chosen from the date, resets at your local midnight, and the same game won't come back within 30 days. Endless mode gives you a fresh rig whenever you want one and keeps its own win streak. You can filter Endless by platform: PC, PS2/Xbox, PS3/Xbox 360, PS4/Xbox One, PS5/Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch, Nintendo Wii, or the handheld Pokémon games on Game Boy/GBA, DS and 3DS.

How scoring works

You get six guesses (eight in Hard mode). Either brand can be right: the weakest AMD and the weakest Intel CPU that reach the target both count, and the same goes for NVIDIA and AMD graphics cards. After each guess, a side view of a PC tower colors each part: green for a correct part, yellow for one step away within its brand, gray for further. An arrow says whether to go stronger or weaker. Each guess also shows its estimated FPS. In normal mode you win with the exact minimum build or any build within 10% of the target. Hard mode only accepts the exact minimum build.

Where the FPS numbers come from

Rigdle uses a deliberately simple model, not real benchmarks. Each of the 47 CPUs and 54 GPUs, from a 2000 Pentium III and GeForce2 up to today's parts, has a relative score based loosely on public benchmark averages (a Ryzen 5 1600 is 100 for CPUs, an RTX 4060 is 100 for GPUs). Each game has a CPU load and a GPU load that turn those scores into frame rates. The build's FPS is the lower of the CPU and GPU numbers, cut by 15% or 50% if you have less RAM than the game recommends. A GPU with less video memory than the game needs loses 40%. Higher resolutions only affect the GPU: about 72% of the 1080p frame rate at 1440p and 42% at 4K, with more video memory required. Everything assumes High settings.

Where real benchmark results exist for a game and a part, Rigdle uses the measured number instead of the estimate, shows its source, and adjusts that game's estimates for untested parts to match.

Console games are estimated as if played on a PC. PS2, Xbox, PS3, Xbox 360, Wii and Switch games are modeled as running through an emulator, which leans heavily on the CPU. PS4- and PS5-era games use their PC port, or an estimated one for exclusives.

The numbers are approximations meant to make a fair puzzle. Real performance depends on drivers, game patches, settings, cooling and the rest of your system, so please don't use Rigdle as a buying guide. See how accurate the FPS model is against published benchmarks.
